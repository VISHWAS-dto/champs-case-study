PHASE 2 - FUNNEL ANALYSIS
=========================

What is a funnel:
- The steps a customer goes through from first contact to paying.
  Fewer people reach each step, so it gets narrower like a funnel.

What I did:
- Counted how many leads reach each step, and how many drop off.
- Script:  analysis/phase_02_funnel_analysis.py
- Results: results/phase_02_funnel_analysis.md (all numbers + formulas)
           results/figures/phase_02/01_funnel_dropoff.png (chart)

The funnel:
    Step              Leads    Moved on from step before   Dropped off
    Lead              5,000    -                           -
    Demo booked       3,229    64.6%                       1,771 never booked
    Demo joined       2,060    63.8%                       1,169 did not show up
    Demo finished     1,786    86.7%                         274 left early
    Paid                362    20.3%                       1,424 did not buy

- Overall: 362 out of 5,000 leads paid = 7.2%.
- Check: 1,771 + 1,169 + 274 + 1,424 + 362 = 5,000. Every lead is counted once.

What I found:
- Most leads are lost at "never booked" (1,771), then "did not buy"
  (1,424), then "did not show up" (1,169).
- The weakest step is after the demo: 8 out of 10 people who finish
  the demo do not pay.
- The demo itself works well: 87% of people who join stay till the end.
- Leads from the last week of July did a little worse (6.6% paid vs 7.3%).
  They may not have had time to finish yet, because we don't know when
  the data was pulled.

Important:
- A drop-off is a count of people, NOT lost money. We cannot multiply
  drop-offs by Rs 60,000, because many of them would never have bought.
- "Did not show up" also includes people who cancelled or moved the demo.
  The data cannot tell these apart.

PHASE 3 - FIND THE LEAK
=======================

What I did:
- Split the funnel by lead source, geography, timezone, rep, shift,
  follow-up attempts, and time from lead to demo.
- Compared every group with everyone else at every step (204 tests),
  and corrected for running so many tests.
- Script:  analysis/phase_03_leak_analysis.py
- Results: results/phase_03_leak_analysis.md
           results/figures/phase_03/ (4 charts)

Three candidate leaks (none picked as final yet):
A. Demo booked more than 48 hours after the lead came in.
   40% of demos. Only 47% of these parents join, vs 75% for demos
   within 48h. Same gap in every group we checked. Strongest finding.
B. India and Vietnam: after a finished demo only 11% pay, vs 23% in
   other countries. Could be price or market, not the sales process.
C. DSA leads: after a finished demo only 11% pay, vs 21% for other
   sources. Weaker: could be chance after 204 tests.

What showed nothing:
- Rep shift, and the parent's local time of the demo.
- Why 35% of leads never book a demo: no column in the file explains it.

PHASE 4 - CHECK THE LEAKS
=========================

What I did:
- Tested the 3 candidate leaks from Phase 3 to see if they hold up.
- For each one, checked if the gap is still there inside every lead
  source, country, timezone, shift, rep, follow-up count, and time period.
- Checked if one small group causes the whole gap, if there is enough
  data, and if other things explain it.
- Used a fair test for "we went looking for this": shuffled the data
  5,000 times and ran the same search, to see how often luck alone
  finds a gap this big.
- Script:  analysis/phase_04_leak_validation.py
- Results: results/phase_04_leak_validation.md
           results/figures/phase_04/ (4 charts)

Results:
A. Demo booked more than 48 hours after the lead came in - STRONG
   - 47% of these parents join, vs 75% for demos within 48 hours.
   - Same gap in every group, both months, every week.
   - These demos are 40% of all demos but 58% of all no-shows.
B. India and Vietnam pay less after the demo - STRONG (but not a sales fix)
   - 11% pay vs 23% elsewhere. Lower than every other country.
   - Same whichever team or rep handles them, so it is likely price or
     market, not something the sales team does wrong.
C. DSA leads pay less after the demo - WEAK
   - Only 20 paying customers in the group.
   - Most of the gap comes from one shift and the first 2 weeks of June.
     Without them, the gap is too small to be sure it is real.

The main problem to fix:
- Demos booked too far out (more than 48 hours). Parents often don't
  show up for them.
- It is the biggest and clearest pattern, it happens everywhere, and the
  sales team controls when demos are booked.

Important:
- The data shows the two things go together. It does NOT prove that
  booking late causes the no-show. Parents who are less keen may simply
  pick later slots. A test (e.g. booking some leads within 48 hours on
  purpose) would be needed to prove it.
