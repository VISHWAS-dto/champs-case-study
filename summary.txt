PHASE 2 - FUNNEL ANALYSIS
=========================

What is a funnel:
- The steps a customer goes through from first contact to paying.
  Fewer people reach each step, so it gets narrower like a funnel.

What I did:
- Counted how many leads reach each step, and how many drop off.
- Script:  analysis/phase_02_funnel_analysis.py
- Results: results/phase_02_funnel_analysis.md (all numbers + formulas)
           results/figures/phase_02/01_funnel_dropoff.png (chart)

The funnel:
    Step              Leads    Moved on from step before   Dropped off
    Lead              5,000    -                           -
    Demo booked       3,229    64.6%                       1,771 never booked
    Demo joined       2,060    63.8%                       1,169 did not show up
    Demo finished     1,786    86.7%                         274 left early
    Paid                362    20.3%                       1,424 did not buy

- Overall: 362 out of 5,000 leads paid = 7.2%.
- Check: 1,771 + 1,169 + 274 + 1,424 + 362 = 5,000. Every lead is counted once.

What I found:
- Most leads are lost at "never booked" (1,771), then "did not buy"
  (1,424), then "did not show up" (1,169).
- The weakest step is after the demo: 8 out of 10 people who finish
  the demo do not pay.
- The demo itself works well: 87% of people who join stay till the end.
- Leads from the last week of July did a little worse (6.6% paid vs 7.3%).
  They may not have had time to finish yet, because we don't know when
  the data was pulled.

Important:
- A drop-off is a count of people, NOT lost money. We cannot multiply
  drop-offs by Rs 60,000, because many of them would never have bought.
- "Did not show up" also includes people who cancelled or moved the demo.
  The data cannot tell these apart.

- Did not work out lost money yet.

Three candidate leaks (none picked as final yet):

Results:
A. Demo booked more than 48 hours after the lead came in - STRONG
   - 47% of these parents join, vs 75% for demos within 48 hours.
   - Same gap in every group, both months, every week.
   - These demos are 40% of all demos but 58% of all no-shows.
B. India and Vietnam pay less after the demo - STRONG (but not a sales fix)
   - 11% pay vs 23% elsewhere. Lower than every other country.
   - Same whichever team or rep handles them, so it is likely price or

C. DSA leads pay less after the demo - WEAK
   - Only 20 paying customers in the group.
   - Most of the gap comes from one shift and the first 2 weeks of June.
     Without them, the gap is too small to be sure it is real.

The main problem to fix:
- Demos booked too far out (more than 48 hours). Parents often don't
  show up for them.
- It is the biggest and clearest pattern, it happens everywhere, and the
  sales team controls when demos are booked.

Important:
- The data shows the two things go together. It does NOT prove that
  booking late causes the no-show. Parents who are less keen may simply
  pick later slots. A test (e.g. booking some leads within 48 hours on

PHASE 5 - HOW MUCH MONEY IS THE LEAK WORTH
==========================================

What I did:
- Put a rupee value on the leak from Phase 4 (demos booked more than
  48 hours after the lead came in).
- Script:  analysis/phase_05_financial_impact.py
- Results: results/phase_05_financial_impact.md (all numbers + formulas)
           results/figures/phase_05/ (3 charts)

The idea (a "what if"):
- 1,285 parents had their demo booked late (more than 48 hours).
- Only 603 of them came to the demo. 682 did not come.
- Parents booked early (within 48 hours) come 75 times out of 100.
- What if the late parents had also come 75 times out of 100?
    1,285 x 75% = about 963 would have come.
    963 - 603 = about 360 extra parents in the demo.
- Those 360 extra parents then go through the normal steps:
    360 joined -> about 310 finish the demo -> about 59 pay.
- 59 extra customers x Rs 60,000 = about Rs 35.4 lakh in 2 months.

The money:
    What                                     2 months       per month
    Money actually earned (362 x 60,000)     Rs 2.17 crore  Rs 1.09 crore
    Marketing spent (5,000 x 900)            Rs 45 lakh     Rs 22.5 lakh
    Best case if the leak is fixed           Rs 35.4 lakh   Rs 17.7 lakh
    Careful estimate (use for planning)      Rs 15.6 lakh   Rs 7.8 lakh
    Money actually lost                      cannot be proven

Why two numbers (best case and careful):
- Best case: assumes booking late is the ONLY reason parents don't come.
- Careful: some parents may pick a late slot because they are less keen,
  and booking them early would not help. So I only count half of the gap,
  and use the low end of the gap's likely range. The "half" is my own
  judgment, not something the data shows.

Where the Rs 900 marketing cost fits:
- It is already spent. Fixing the leak needs no new leads, so it is NOT
  taken away from the extra money.
- It shows the cost of getting each paying customer: Rs 12,431 today,
  about Rs 10,687 if the full leak were fixed.
- Getting the same 59 extra customers by buying more leads would cost
  about Rs 7.3 lakh of marketing. Fixing the leak gets them without that.

Why NOT "every non-buyer x Rs 60,000":
- 1,162 late parents did not buy. 1,162 x 60,000 = Rs 6.97 crore.
- That is wrong. Most of them would not have bought anyway. Even among
  parents booked early, only about 12 out of 100 end up paying.

Why the actual loss cannot be proven:
- The data shows late booking and no-shows go together, not that one
  causes the other.
- Rs 60,000 is money coming in, not profit.
- We don't know what the fix would cost to run.

PHASE 6 - IDEAS TO FIX THE LEAK
===============================

What I did:
- Wrote 5 possible fixes for the leak (demos booked more than 48 hours
  after the lead came in). Did NOT pick one yet.
- For each fix: what it solves, how it works, steps, time to launch,
  tech and team needed, AI use, risks, how to measure it, main downside.
- Script:  analysis/phase_06_intervention_charts.py
- Results: results/phase_06_intervention_options.md
           results/figures/phase_06/ (4 charts)

Useful facts from the data:
- About 21 demos a day are booked late. That is fewer than 2 per rep per
  day (12 reps), so the team can handle them by hand.
- Parents come about 75 times out of 100 when the demo is within 48 hours.
  After 48 hours it drops to about 47, and it does NOT keep falling.
  A demo 3 days away and one 7 days away do about the same.
- The team can book fast: half of the early demos happen within 15 hours.

Two possible reasons for the leak (the data can't tell which):
1. The process: something changes at 48 hours, e.g. reminders are only
   sent for demos within 48 hours. Then a fix should work well.
2. The parents: less keen parents pick late slots. Then a fix helps less.

The 5 fixes:
    Fix                                     Type          Ready in       Fits 2 weeks?
    1. Book demos within 48h by default;    Process       3-5 days       Yes
       a later slot needs a reason
    2. WhatsApp reminders for demos         Messaging     7-10 days      Yes
       booked more than 48h out
    3. Daily sheet of late demos; reps      Spreadsheet   3-5 days       Yes
       call to move them earlier
    4. Score that ranks which demos may     Scoring       4-6 days       Yes, but adds
       be no-shows                                                       little
    5. AI chat on WhatsApp that confirms    AI agent      2-3 weeks      Probably not
       and moves demos earlier

Main catch for each:
1. Reps may just write "parent asked" every time. Needs free early slots.
2. Won't help if late demos already get the same reminders. Check first.
3. Reps may skip the calls on busy days. Needs free early slots.
4. There is almost nothing to rank: once past 48 hours, all late demos
   are about the same. A simple "more than 48h" flag does the same job.
5. Slowest and riskiest. Needs system connections and legal checks.

How we would know a fix works:
- Before starting, note today's numbers: 40% of demos booked late,
  47% of late-booked parents come to the demo.
- Give the fix to half of the late demos and not the other half, then
  compare how many parents come.
- With about 21 late demos a day, this needs about 3.5 to 4 weeks to
  spot an improvement the size of the careful estimate from Phase 5.

PHASE 7 - CHOOSING THE FIX
==========================

What I did:
- Compared the 5 fixes from Phase 6 and picked ONE.
- Explained why the other 4 were not picked.
- Made 4 charts.
- Script:  analysis/phase_07_lever_charts.py
- Results: results/phase_07_selected_lever.md
           results/figures/phase_07/ (4 charts)

The fix I picked: the daily "Late-Demo Rescue" list
- Every morning, each shift gets a list of demos that were booked more
  than 48 hours after the parent first came in.
- The rep calls or WhatsApps the parent and offers an earlier slot.
- If the parent can't move, the rep just confirms the booked time.
- Only half the list gets a call. The other half is left alone so we
  can compare the two and see if the calls really help.

One important detail:
- The new slot must be within 48 hours of when the parent FIRST came in.
- Moving a demo from day 5 to day 3 does not help. Parents show up
  about 45-50 times out of 100 anywhere after 48 hours.
- So each row on the list has a deadline, and reps must act fast.

Why this fix:
- Ready in about 5 working days. No engineers needed, just a
  spreadsheet and the data export the company already has.
- Small workload: about 1 call per rep per day, around 5 minutes.
- It helps whatever the real reason for no-shows turns out to be
  (the wait, or less keen parents).
- It is easy to measure fairly (called half vs not-called half).
- We can build a working version of it using the case data.

Why not the others:
1. Booking rule:  Reps can dodge it by writing "parent asked" every
                  time. Hard to measure fairly.
2. Reminders:     Only works if late demos get fewer reminders today,
                  and nobody has checked that yet. WhatsApp approval
                  could delay it.
3. Risk score:    Nothing to rank. After 48 hours all late demos look
                  the same.
4. AI agent:      Too slow (over 2 weeks), needs engineers, and a wrong
                  message to a parent could hurt the brand.

What it could be worth (per month):
- If the calls lift show-ups on late demos by about 12 points
  (47 -> 59 out of 100): about Rs 7.8 lakh a month.
- If they close the whole gap (47 -> 75): about Rs 17.7 lakh a month.
- Each extra point of show-up is worth about Rs 63,000 a month.

Biggest guess (not proven):
- That the waiting itself makes parents skip the demo. If parents who
  pick late slots were simply less keen, calling them helps less.

Biggest risk:
- Reps are busy and skip the calls, or call too late.
- To catch this, we check daily that at least 80 out of 100 rows are
  worked before their deadline.

How we will know it worked:
- Run it for 4 weeks. Compare show-ups: called half vs untouched half.
- Called half clearly better -> keep it and grow it.
- No difference (and reps did the calls) -> stop, try something else.
- Reps didn't do the calls -> fix that first, then judge.

What changed:
- Money: Rs 7.8 lakh a month is now called the value of the leak. What
  the fix itself captures is lower: about +3 points of join rate, about
  Rs 1.9 lakh a month, using rates that are guesses (80% worked in time
  x 60% parents reached x 50% accept an earlier slot). Phase 5 sec 10-11.
- Comparison: every big funnel drop is sized the same way. Never-booked
  and left-early have no gap to close; India + Vietnam (Rs 17.0 lakh)
  is similar in size but looks like price, not sales process.
- Test rule: scale if the low end of the range is above 0; stop only if
  the high end is below +3 points; otherwise keep testing. The main
  comparison uses demos that could still be moved (3h or more left).
- Adjustment: now uses only facts known when the lead arrives. Result
  OR 0.29 (was 0.27 with follow-ups, which can be a result of a late demo).
- Wording: "demo slot more than 48h after the lead", not "booked more
  than 48h". Day 1 reminder-rule check, Day 3 slot check (go/no-go),
  displacement guardrail (late share stays at or below 39.8%).
- Tool: MOVE only with 3h or more left; a first-seen register keeps each
  demo in its group even after it is moved; "Load sample data" button
  with made-up data; runbook of who does what in prototype/README.md.
- Repo: the case PDF and CSV are no longer in the repository.
