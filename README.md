## The leak

The funnel: **lead → demo scheduled → joined → completed → paid**. There are 5,000 leads (1 Jun – 31 Jul 2026), and 362 of them pay (7.2%). `demo_scheduled_at` is treated as the **demo slot**, not the time the booking was made. The file has no booking timestamp.

| | Demos | Join rate (J/S) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Slot ≤ 48h after lead creation | 1,944 | **74.9%** |
| Slot > 48h after lead creation | 1,285 (39.8%) | **46.9%** |
| Difference | | **−28.0 pp** (95% CI −31.3 to −24.7) |

- **A step, not a slope.** The join rate is 74–80% in every 12h bin up to 48h, then 42–53% and flat.
- **Present in every subgroup:** source, country, shift, rep, follow-up count, half-month and week. Dropping any single group moves the gap by at most 1.6 pp.
- **Survives adjustment for pre-exposure variables.** These are source, geography, rep, month and lead weekday: OR 0.29 [0.25, 0.34]. Follow-up attempts are left out of this model because they can be a consequence of a far-off demo. Adding them (sensitivity only) gives OR 0.27.
- **Not a search artefact.** The 48h cut was found by scanning, so the scan was re-run on 5,000 shuffled datasets. The observed |z| was 16.2 against a 95th percentile of 2.65.
- **Concentrated at the join step.** These demos hold 58% of all no-shows. Parents who do join complete (87.9%) and convert (23.2%) at least as well as everyone else.
- **Still a correlation.** Less-keen parents may simply choose later slots. An unmeasured confounder would need a risk ratio ≥ 2.57 with both late slots and no-shows (E-value) to explain the gap. Only the pilot's holdout tests cause.

**Funnel drops on a common basis.** Each stage is sized by closing the gap to a benchmark observed in the same file (`results/phase_05_financial_impact.md` §10):

| Stage | Lost at this step | Benchmark gap | Modeled ceiling / month | Selected? |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| Never booked | 1,771 leads | None: S/L 57–67% in every segment | not sizable | No: largest raw drop, but nothing in the data to target |
| **No-show, slot > 48h** | 1,285 demos | **28.0 pp** vs ≤ 48h | **₹17.7 lakh** (₹7.8 lakh causal) | **Yes** |
| Left demo early | 274 joiners | None: C/J 81–89% | not sizable | No: small and flat |
| No purchase, India + Vietnam | 464 completers | 12.2 pp vs other geographies | ₹17.0 lakh | No: same across every rep and shift, which points to price or market fit |

The late-demo leak is the one that sales operations can move within two weeks. The team decides which slot a parent is offered, the affected demos can be identified on the day they are booked, and about 21 a day can be worked by the existing reps from a daily CRM export.

## Financial impact

```
Planning case (causal value of the leak):
1,285 late demos × 24.65 pp (low end of CI) × 50% causal share × 16.4% customers per on-time joiner
  ≈ 26 customers × ₹60,000 = ₹15.6 lakh / 2 months ≈ ₹7.8 lakh/month
Ceiling: the whole 28.0 pp gap closes → 59 customers → ₹35.4 lakh / 2 months ≈ ₹17.7 lakh/month
```

- **The 50% causal share is a judgment, not a measurement.** At 25% the planning case is ₹3.9 lakh/month. Using the low end of the CI covers sampling noise; the causal share covers confounding.
- **16.4%** = on-time joiners' C/J × V/C. It is conservative, since late joiners convert at 20.4%.
- **Revenue, not profit.** This is a modeled opportunity, not a proven loss. The ₹900 per lead is already spent, so it is not deducted.

**Leak value ≠ lever capture.** The lever only reaches rescue rows worked before the deadline, and only where the parent accepts an earlier slot. The rates below are **illustrative assumptions, not observed data**; the pilot measures them.

```
expected lift = reach × acceptance × effect on a moved demo
              = (80% worked in time × 60% parent reached) × 50% accept × 12.3 pp
              ≈ +3.0 pp on all late demos ≈ ₹1.9 lakh/month at full rollout (≈ ₹63,000 per pp)
```

## The lever: daily Late-Demo Rescue list

1. Each shift gets a list of upcoming demos whose slot is more than 48h after lead creation.
2. The owning rep calls or WhatsApps the parent and offers a slot **before `created_at + 48h`**. If less than 3h remains, the row shows CONFIRM instead of MOVE, and the rep confirms the current slot live.
3. Leads with an odd ID go to reps (rescue arm). Leads with an even ID are held out untouched (control arm).

**Why this one:** it acts on exactly the population and the boundary where the gap sits. The load is about 1 call per rep per day. It is **informative whichever explanation is true**: moving the demo tests the wait, and the call reveals the parent's intent. It can go live in about 5 working days with no engineering, **assuming a daily CRM export with the 12 case columns can be pulled and there are free demo slots inside 48h.**

**Rejected:** a 48h booking rule (easy to game, changes the reps' core task, hard to measure fairly) · WhatsApp reminders (only help if late demos get fewer reminders today, which is unchecked; template approval puts a 2-week launch at risk) · a no-show risk score (nothing to rank beyond the 48h flag) · an AI rebooking agent (more than 2 weeks, needs CRM/calendar integration, highest brand risk). Full reasoning: `results/phase_06_intervention_options.md`, `results/phase_07_selected_lever.md`.

**Go-live gates:**

- **Day 1:** check the CRM's existing reminder rules. If reminders only fire for demos within 48h, fix that rule first, since it may explain the step.
- **Day 3:** capacity check, as a go/no-go. Are there free slots inside 48h in every shift? If a shift has none, the list becomes confirmation-only there.

## Pilot and measurement

The pilot is **randomised from day one; the readout is at week 4.** That gives about 300 late demos per arm.

- **Primary:** join rate (J/S), rescue minus control, intent-to-treat, 95% CI, in the **MOVE-eligible stratum**: late demos whose deadline was still ≥ 3h away when first listed. All listed late demos are reported as the secondary readout.
- **Power:** 300 per arm gives about 80% power only for an effect of about 11 pp. The power for +3 pp is about 11%.
- **Decision rule, set in advance.** The minimum worthwhile effect (MWE) is **+3 pp**.
  - **Scale** if the lower bound of the 95% CI is above 0.
  - **Stop** only if the upper bound of the 95% CI is below +3 pp.
  - **Otherwise extend.** At week 4 the most likely result for a small true effect is *extend*, by design.
- **Adoption guardrail:** at least 80% of rescue rows worked before their deadline. Below that, fix adoption before reading the result.
- **Other guardrails:**
  - C/J and V/C of rescue-arm joiners do not fall below control.
  - **Displacement:** the share of new bookings with a slot more than 48h out stays at or below the **39.8%** baseline.
- **Baseline (Jun–Jul 2026):**
  - late share 39.8%
  - J/S late 46.9% (primary)
  - J/S ≤ 48h 74.9%
  - V/S late 9.6%
  - C/J and V/C of late joiners 87.9% / 23.2%
  - A/A check of the arms on history: −1.0 pp (CI −6.4 to +4.5)

## Prototype

`prototype/rescue_sheet.html` plus `rescue_logic.js`. It is a single offline page with no install, no dependencies and no network calls, and the data stays in the browser.

- It loads the daily CRM export and flags upcoming late demos. It computes each rescue deadline in the parent's local time and marks each row **MOVE** (≥ 3h left) or **CONFIRM**.
- It splits rows into rescue (odd ID) and control (even ID). Reps only see the rescue arm, sorted by deadline, with a call script and a one-click outcome log.
- It keeps a **first-seen register**, so each demo's arm and MOVE-eligibility are fixed when it is first listed. A demo moved earlier in the CRM no longer looks late, and without the register it would silently drop out of the rescue arm.
- **Summary** tab: % of rows worked per shift and rep. **Measurement** tab: the primary and secondary readouts and the week-4 verdict.
- **Load sample data** runs the whole flow on synthetic data with one click.

The operating runbook (who exports, who merges logs, who pulls results) is in [`prototype/README.md`](prototype/README.md#operating-owner).

## Assumptions and limits

- **Given by the case:** ₹60,000 revenue per customer and ₹900 per lead.
- **Timestamps:**
  - `demo_scheduled_at` is the demo slot, and a blank means no demo was booked.
  - The export clock is assumed to be UTC. Deadlines are differences between two timestamps, so they don't depend on this; the displayed local times do.
  - The timestamps are spread evenly across all 24 hours, so they **cannot** show which clock is used. For the same reason, time-of-day patterns can't be reliably interpreted from this dataset.
- **Operations (all unverified):**
  - A daily CRM export with the 12 case columns is available before each shift.
  - Reps are available in every shift (5 US, 5 IST, 2 SEA).
  - A call takes about 5 minutes.
  - Each shift can reach its parents during that shift's hours.
  - **Slot capacity inside 48h is unknown**; the Day-3 go/no-go gate checks it.
- **Financial model:**
  - Extra joiners complete and convert at the on-time baseline rates.
  - The 50% causal share and the capture rates are judgments.
- **What the data cannot tell us:**
  - cause
  - actual loss or profit
  - why 35% of leads never book
  - why India and Vietnam convert less
  - today's reminder rules
  - cancellations vs no-shows
  - the extract date: late-July leads may still be mid-funnel
